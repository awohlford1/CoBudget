## 1. Purpose and authority

This document is the initial Private MVP inventory and classification baseline
required by CBD-91. It inventories primary and derived customer data plus the
copies created in caches, queues, notifications, exports, logs, analytics,
backups, and support operations. It is an input to CBD-92 trust-boundary
modeling and CBD-93 privacy and abuse-case analysis; it is not a production
schema, legal conclusion, retention schedule, or security certification.

The approved CBD-11/CBD-71 financial and schedule decisions and the Product
Owner-approved decisions in CBD-12/CBD-72 are authoritative product inputs.
**MVP Schedule Decisions v1.1 is the authoritative CBD-71 decision set**,
approved by the Product Owner on August 15, 2026; v1.0 is retained history.
CBD-72 inherits v1.1 under its closed decision OD-72-06, and all six CBD-72 open
decisions are closed. This inventory may identify a security requirement,
evidence gap, or source conflict but does not silently change an approved
outcome. Provider names in `docs/architecture.md` are starting hypotheses until
CBD-15 selects the managed provider set. Section 7 records the source precedence
applied wherever current documents disagree.

## 2. Inventory conventions

### 2.1 Sensitivity

| Level | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S4 — Secret | Credential or bearer material whose disclosure can directly enable account, session, provider, or protected-action compromise. | Session secrets, provider access tokens, invitation bearer tokens, export keys |
| S3 — Restricted | Financial, authentication, security, or highly sensitive personal data whose disclosure could cause financial harm, surveillance, coercion, or material loss of control. | Transactions, balances, financial plans/goals, comments/free text, alert events, MFA evidence, customer-data job/diagnostic state, detailed audit/security events |
| S2 — Sensitive | Personal, collaboration, planning, or operational data that can identify people, relationships, intentions, or private product use. | Contact/display profiles, shared settings, invitation or delivery-status metadata, content-free deletion tombstones and lifecycle receipts |
| S1 — Internal | Non-public operational metadata that should not expose customer content or privileged detection logic. | Allowlisted content-free operational logs, aggregate health metrics, non-secret service/key version metadata |
| S0 — Public | Intentionally public content. | Public Mission/About content; not otherwise inventoried here |

Classification is based on the most sensitive field normally present in the
class. Derived and copied data inherit the highest sensitivity of any input;
aggregation, masking, hashing, encryption, or pseudonymization does not lower
the classification without a separate documented release decision.

### 2.2 Data stewardship and audience

“Subject” means the person the data describes; “author” means the person who
created an interaction; “authorizer” means the individual who consented to a
specific financial connection; and “budget space” means the shared customer
context. Product role abbreviations are PO (Primary Owner), CO (Co-owner), C
(Collaborator), V (Viewer within current profile), and AP (Accountability
Partner within its comprehensive financially read-only boundary).

“Source” identifies where a fact or record originates. “System steward” names
the application domain or service accountable for the authoritative application
copy, authorization boundary, integrity, and lifecycle. It does not assert legal
ownership, transfer a person's rights in their data, make a budget space a legal
person, or override the rights and obligations of subjects, authorizers,
recipients, providers, CoBudget, or other parties. Product roles named Primary
Owner and Co-owner are permission roles only and do not establish legal title to
another person's data.

The authorized audience column describes customer-facing application access.
It does not grant support staff, engineers, vendors, or administrators access.
Operational access remains default-deny and is an evidence gap where not yet
specified.

### 2.3 Lifecycle notation

* **Retain/history** means product rules require a durable historical record,
  but duration and deletion propagation remain `EG-91-001` unless stated.
* **Account lifecycle** means retention follows an unapproved personal-account
  deletion design (`EG-91-002`).
* **Budget lifecycle** means retention follows the unresolved budget-space
  deletion design (`EG-91-003`).
* An evidence-gap ID is an explicit unknown, not permission to choose a value
  during implementation.

## 3. Data inventory and classification matrix

A class is split when its fields require materially different audiences,
administrative access, lifecycle actions, or storage boundaries. Fields that
share those controls remain together and receive the highest applicable
sensitivity; an accidental higher-sensitivity value is a control violation, not
a lower-classification exception.

Rows are grouped by functional domain and sorted by stable ID within each
domain. A numerical gap means that a later refinement was assigned a new stable
ID and displayed with its functional domain; IDs are never renumbered, reused,
or made semantically dependent on display order.

## 4. Required noninterference and copy rules

1. Every data class and copy location is governed by catalog/schema metadata for
   source/provenance, sensitivity, lifecycle and system stewardship. Each record,
   job or cache entry carries the budget-space, subject/resource, provenance and
   authorization/version identifiers actually needed to prevent cross-space or
   stale use; this does not require a duplicated sensitivity label on every row.
2. User-delegated work is authorized before search, aggregation, report
   calculation, rendering, caching, export, snapshot generation, recipient-bound
   payload construction, or queueing, and current authorization is rechecked
   before any delayed mutation or disclosure. Filtering only the final UI or
   relying only on authorization captured when a job was created is insufficient.
3. Counts, totals, labels, graph shape, timing, validation errors, suggestions,
   empty states, and the existence of a job/package/alert must not reveal a
   hidden resource.
4. Revocation, role/profile/scope change, ownership transfer, target removal, or
   authorization-version change immediately invalidates affected user-delegated
   reads, mutations, calculations, caches, indexes, deliveries, downloads,
   snapshots, alert eligibility and pending jobs. A worker must suppress or
   recompute stale user-facing work rather than deliver its old result.
5. Logs, analytics, support tools, audit payloads, and vendor dashboards are
   separate disclosure surfaces; exclusion from a product screen does not make
   a field safe to copy there.
6. Free text and provider payloads are treated as containing S3 data unless a
   narrower schema and redaction boundary are proven.
7. App-controlled client copies must expire or purge when their authorization is
   no longer current. CoBudget must not claim that revoking access, expiring a
   server package, or deleting server data retracts a notification, downloaded
   export, screenshot, printout, or other copy already controlled by a recipient
   or their device.
8. Security, fraud/risk, audit, revocation, deletion, retention-enforcement and
   restoration-reconciliation jobs may continue after user authority is removed
   only under an explicit narrow service-authority purpose. They use the minimum
   data needed to enforce or prove the lifecycle/security event and may perform
   only the mutation expressly required by that allowlisted purpose, such as
   revocation, deletion, retention enforcement, or restoration reconciliation.
   They cannot perform an unrelated product mutation or customer disclosure
   except the separately authorized safe status, and cannot be repurposed as a
   general bypass of product permissions.
9. Every queued job records its authority mode and purpose. User-delegated jobs
   bind to the initiating subject/recipient, resource scope and authorization
   version; service-authority jobs bind to an allowlisted system purpose and
   service policy version. Missing, ambiguous or stale authority fails closed.
10. A lifecycle threshold evaluated on one person's behaviour must not become an
    inference oracle about that person. Evaluate it server-side, persist the
    coarsest state that satisfies the rule rather than the underlying precise
    signal, expose no countdown or pre-request eligibility indicator, return a
    uniform result that does not distinguish ineligible from any other refusal,
    rate-limit attempts per requester, audit every attempt, and notify the
    subject of each attempt so that observation of a person is never covert.

### 7.1 Material account-authority and stewardship decision

`CR-91-008` is the only conflict found that prevents a stable system-stewardship model
inside this inventory. The candidate dispositions are:

1. **Financial-profile authority with explicit budget links (recommended):** a
   person-level financial-profile domain is authoritative system steward for each
   individual's connection and canonical provider/account identity; a versioned,
   revocable link exposes a
   safe account representation to a budget space. Consent, tokens, cursors,
   source observations, revocation state, and provenance remain separate for
   every independently authorized provider connection. When reliable provider
   identity or explicit confirmation establishes an overlap, those observations
   may reference one normalized account or transaction for presentation and
   duplicate prevention, but the normalized record retains reversible provenance
   edges to every contributing connection and never inherits one connection's
   authority for another. Each budget-space domain is system steward for its
   planning classifications, visibility, comments, alerts, and other
   space-specific overlays.
2. **Budget-space-scoped authoritative account copies:** every space maintains
   a separate authoritative account and transaction copy, even when the same
   person/provider account appears in
   several spaces. This simplifies tenant reads but multiplies provider events,
   deduplication, corrections, deletion, provenance, and consistency risks.
3. **Automatic global profile visibility (rejected safe-position candidate):**
   connecting an account makes it available to every current or future budget
   space. This contradicts explicit, private, cross-budget isolation and must
   not be used.

Option 1 best reconciles CBD-67 with CBD-72, but it is not approved by this
document. Product Owner approval and CBD-82/CBD-12 data-model evidence are
required before `EG-91-021` closes.

### 7.2 Interim account-unlink and provider-disconnection historical-retention policy

The Product Owner approved the following working policy on August 15, 2026,
subject to `EG-91-001–003` and the mandatory pre-launch legal and compliance
review in `EG-91-022`:

1. **Unlinking an account from a budget space** removes the explicit account-to-space
   link. It immediately stops new source data and derived updates from being
   routed to that space, invalidates pending work authorized only by that link,
   and ends future account access for that space subject to the retained-history
   rule below. It does not revoke provider authorization or stop a provider
   connection that remains independently authorized for the financial profile or
   another linked budget space.
2. **Disconnecting a provider connection** terminates that specific connection.
   It immediately stops future collection and synchronization through the
   connection and revokes or destroys its provider authorization material as
   supported by the provider. It does not terminate another independently
   authorized connection, and its existing historical records remain governed by
   the retention and deletion rules below.
3. Following an unlink, historical imported transactions may remain visible in
   the affected budget space only where retention is necessary to preserve the
   shared budget history the user explicitly requested and the sharing
   disclosure stated that result clearly. The UI identifies the retained record
   as historical and no longer synchronizing for that space. Following a
   connection disconnection, that presentation applies only when no other active
   authorized connection continues to supply the record to that space; otherwise
   CoBudget recomputes the normalized record and health state from the remaining
   connection-specific provenance without implying that synchronization stopped.
   Provider-supplied source facts and provenance remain immutable in either case.
4. Space-specific overlays on an approved retained transaction—such as category,
   budget-date override, reconciliation decision, comment, and their versioned
   history—may continue to be created, edited, or removed only under the current
   permissions and lifecycle of that budget space. An overlay change cannot alter
   provider-source facts, reactivate synchronization, recreate an account link,
   expand another role's visibility, or be represented as a provider correction.
5. Retained history remains limited to the same budget space and current
   authorized roles. It cannot be reused for advertising, unrelated analytics,
   cross-selling, sale, or another budget space.
6. Neither unlinking nor disconnecting is itself a deletion request. The user
   receives a separate deletion-request path, and verified deletion requests
   follow the approved lifecycle, legal exceptions, provider duties, and
   copy-propagation controls.
7. Authorization, revocation, audit, and legal-compliance evidence is minimized
   and stored separately where practical; retaining that evidence does not
   justify retaining full transaction payloads.
8. Before confirmation, the user experience identifies whether the action is a
   budget-space unlink or a provider disconnection, its exact scope, the data
   that will remain, the people or roles that may continue to see it, the effect
   on future synchronization and other linked spaces, which retained fields are
   immutable or remain editable, and how to request deletion.
9. Retention is not indefinite. `EG-91-001` must establish an approved period,
   trigger, accountable lifecycle steward, deletion SLA, and verification method
   before launch.
10. Pre-launch legal and compliance review must analyze the exact customer
   disclosures, former-member and joint-account cases, deletion rights and
   exceptions, financial-data access/revocation rules, state-law applicability,
   required compliance-record retention, provider contracts, and supported
   launch jurisdictions. Required changes must be resolved before launch.

This working policy is a product and safety constraint, not legal advice or a
finding that any specific retention period or disclosure is compliant.

### 7.3 Inactive-owner archival eligibility disclosure policy

The Product Owner approved the following policy on August 15, 2026. CBD-72 §6.3
requires a 90-day Primary-inactivity threshold that any active Co-owner or
Collaborator may act on, which necessarily makes a judgement about one
identified person's behaviour available to others. That decision is approved and
is not reopened here. This policy bounds how the supporting data is stored and
disclosed, and resolves `CR-91-012`.

1. **No pre-request visibility.** No member sees an eligibility state, a
   countdown, a last-active time, or any other indicator that the threshold is
   approaching or has been crossed. The request affordance is available to every
   entitled role at all times and the server decides.
2. **Coarse storage.** `DI-91-076` persists only a derived eligibility state
   that stays null until the threshold is crossed. The precise last-activity
   time is not persisted in that class, so a leak, IDOR, backup, restore, or
   export exposure discloses eligibility rather than a behavioural timeline.
3. **Uniform, rate-limited responses.** An ineligible request returns a result
   that does not distinguish ineligibility from any other refusal and reveals no
   date, remaining interval, or prior attempt. Attempts are rate-limited per
   requester per budget space, and every attempt is audited. Together these
   prevent the endpoint from being polled to pin the threshold date and thereby
   recover the Primary Owner's last-activity date.
4. **Mandatory notice to the subject.** The Primary Owner is notified of every
   archival-request attempt whether or not it was eligible, and receives advance
   notice as the threshold approaches. These notices are addressed to the
   subject of the judgement and cannot be suppressed by another member's
   preferences. Observation of a person is never covert.
5. **Narrow definition of activity.** Qualifying activity is any authenticated
   interaction with that budget space attributable to the Primary Owner. It
   excludes background token refresh and is never derived from `DI-91-053`
   device, network, or security-risk evidence.
6. **Per-space and single-purpose.** The state is scoped to one budget space and
   is never combined across spaces, surfaced as presence, or reused for
   analytics, support, product, or any purpose other than §6.3 eligibility.
7. **No history.** Only the current state is kept. It resets on qualifying
   Primary Owner activity and is purged on archival, restoration, ownership
   transfer, or space deletion. Evidence of a request lives in `DI-91-009`.
8. **Accepted residual.** Once a request is made, the notification required by
   OD-72-01 tells every active member that the Primary Owner was inactive for at
   least the threshold period. That disclosure is inherent to the approved
   decision. It is recorded as an accepted residual rather than designed away,
   and CBD-93 must model the coercion and surveillance cases it creates.

This policy does not approve the 90-day or 14-day parameters, which CBD-72 owns
and may tune, and it does not substitute for the CBD-93 abuse-case analysis.
