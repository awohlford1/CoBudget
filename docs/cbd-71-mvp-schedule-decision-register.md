## 1. Purpose and authority

This register consolidates the approved schedule, income, transaction-classification, validation, and scenario-governance decisions from CBD-67 through CBD-70. Once the Product Owner approves version 1.0, decisions with status **Accepted for MVP** become authoritative for design, technical specification, implementation, and testing.

The current 0.2 revision is a proposed consolidation. It does not replace the approved source specifications and does not itself record Product Owner approval. If this register conflicts with a frozen source before approval, the frozen source controls and the conflict must be resolved here rather than silently interpreted.

For CBD-71, validated means Product Owner approved, internally consistent, implementation-ready, and architecture-aligned. It does not mean customer or market validated.

## 3. Register conventions

### 3.1 Status and classification

Allowed decision statuses are **Proposed**, **Accepted for MVP**, **Deferred**, **Unresolved**, and **Superseded**. Classification states whether the behavior belongs to MVP, deferred scope, or unresolved scope. A proposed MVP decision becomes Accepted for MVP only after every validation gate passes and Product Owner approval is recorded.

### 3.2 Common ownership and approval fields

Unless a row says otherwise:

* Decision owner: Alexander Wohlford — Product Owner.

* Status: Proposed.

These common values are part of every row. Approval may be recorded only after the Product Owner reviews this exact revision.

### 3.3 Implication codes

### 3.4 Determinism and evidence governance

The following rules govern every decision and scenario; they are validation controls rather than separate product decisions:

* The budget's named IANA time zone governs local dates and local midnight.
* Canonical periods use `[startDate, exclusiveEndDate)`; inclusive display ends are derived rather than stored as a second source of truth.
* Dates, date-times, time zones, and currencies use locale-independent ISO and IANA representations.
* Holiday calculations use explicit, versioned reference data rather than a live mutable service.
* Calculations expose inputs, formulas, intermediate values where material, rounding, and reconciliation equations.
* Scenarios state both intended changes and protected non-changes.
* Commands, imported events, scheduled events, derived calculations, security events, and administrative events remain distinct.
* Equivalent event sequences converge on the same final financial state, and exact event replays are idempotent.

These controls consolidate former SD-071-018 and the replay rule formerly grouped into SD-071-014. Their acceptance is evaluated through the validation checklist rather than as user-facing behavior.

## 7. Cross-decision consistency findings

* CBD-70’s approved no-carry-forward rule supersedes older CBD-70 description language requesting accepted and declined positive rollover examples. SD-071-028 records the accepted behavior; DD-071-003 records the deferral.
* Target-and-tracking is authoritative. No accepted decision creates an available-to-assign balance or income-allocation step.
* Occurrence exceptions affect income projections only. Only a confirmed schedule change may alter canonical boundaries.
* Late financial adjustments may change ended-period totals but never the period’s boundaries or schedule-version reference.
* Collaborator schedule and reconciliation permissions are consistent across the current approved CBD-67 through CBD-70 baseline. CBD-12 remains a dependency for the broader role model.
* Product-visible pending-to-posted states and manual controls are MVP behavior; confidence-score tuning is deferred and cannot remove those controls.

## 8. Change control

1. Accepted and superseded records are retained; they are never deleted or silently rewritten.
2. A material change creates a new register revision and names the decision it supersedes.
3. The change record identifies affected CBD-1, CBD-11, CBD-67 through CBD-71 criteria, scenario IDs, data-model fields, interfaces, alerts, audit events, and tests.
4. Jira requirements, repository artifacts, and the published register are updated together.
5. A source-version change requires an impact assessment before its behavior enters this register.
6. Editorial corrections may retain the decision status only when the impact review proves that no expected outcome changed.
